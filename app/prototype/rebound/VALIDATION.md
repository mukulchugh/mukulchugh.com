# Rebound homepage tile and prototype

Homepage: http://localhost:3000 · isolated preview: http://localhost:3000/prototype/rebound

Shared implementation lives in `components/rebound/` and uses the approved asset pack through Next Image. Homepage replaces the motion-study artwork with “For the playful ones.” beside the profile tile and above the location tile. The isolated prototype reuses this component; only that preview route returns notFound in production.

Below 768px the rink rotates vertically, with the player at the bottom and opponent at the top. Sprite positions, mouse/touch coordinates and arrow keys map into the unchanged simulation. Labels, scores and overlay controls remain upright. Mobile rink height is viewport-bounded; Play/Resume brings the tile into view to keep the paddle away from the floating dock. Resize pauses play. The homepage keeps one main h1; the game title is h2.

Homepage integration checks cover 1440, 768, 390 and 320px: orientation, single h1, screen-relative arrow movement, mouse input, emulated touch dragging, pause-panel containment, reset and overflow. Full-page evidence is `.scratch/rebound/home-{width}.png`; hero and tile captures are in the same folder. Compact overlays use container width so tablet bento columns do not wrap pause controls outside the rink. Physical-device Safari/Android testing remains outstanding. No production build was run against the user-owned development server.

First load shows an automated two-paddle rally without an overlay or demo caption. The unobstructed Play button starts a fresh user-controlled match. An accessible pause/resume icon controls the demo independently. Demo simulation suspends off-screen, in hidden tabs and on blur; reduced-motion users see a still board until they choose Play. `bun scripts/check-rebound-demo.ts` covers both paddles moving, no initial overlay, pause/resume, focus suspension, takeover and reduced motion.

Controls: Play, hover the cursor to move, drag the lime paddle on touch, or use arrow keys. Escape/Pause, Resume and Restart are available. First to five. Goals automatically start a three-second next-serve countdown; a stationary puck resets after three seconds without changing the score. The board overlay shows countdowns and pause/finished states. Pause freezes the serve countdown. Own goals count on both sides. User matches pause on blur, hidden tab, viewport resize and when the rink leaves view. Reduced-motion mode slows essential gameplay; it does not eliminate puck travel. Overlay buttons use slimmer, tracked labels and retain 44px tap targets.

Rink: simulation is 1000 × 500, up from 1000 × 385. Goal openings remain 121 units high, centered vertically. The new 2:1 rink artwork adds vertical space without stretching the center circle. Game content ends at the scoreboard; the site-wide footer remains.

Opponent: predicts wall-bank interceptions, lines up deliberate shots at an exposed goal area, and sometimes attempts bank shots. Three styles change pace and defensive depth. Timed recovery windows soften pressure. Reaction delay, bounded acceleration and aiming error prevent perfect tracking. Reads paddle position, not cursor input. Style history persists across serves; each match gets a new random seed.

Checks: `bunx tsc --noEmit`, scoped Biome, and `bun scripts/check-rebound.ts`, `bun scripts/check-rebound-goals.ts`, `bun scripts/check-rebound-deadlock.ts`, `bun scripts/check-rebound-opponent.ts`, `bun scripts/check-rebound-serve.ts`. Assertions cover both goals and own goals, sidewall reflection, three-second recovery, acceleration limits, intentional attacks on stationary and slow incoming pucks, style changes and recovery windows. Browser checks cover cursor and keyboard input, automatic serves, pause/resume/restart, page errors, overflow and pause-panel containment at 1440, 390 and 320 pixels. Screenshots: `.scratch/rebound/`.

Proactive-attack regression: nine passive-player scenarios cover loose center-line pucks and moving-away pucks across three seeded styles. The previous attack zone excluded center-line opportunities, while a tight wind-up tolerance made the opponent trail moving pucks without swinging. The opponent now predicts a short contact lead and allows running strikes, with a 540-unit center limit that keeps its paddle on its own half. These checks assert actual outgoing puck velocity, not only attack-mode selection.

150 scripted opening shots on the taller field: old chaser conceded 13, returned 122, left 15 unresolved; new opponent conceded 1, returned 149, left 0 unresolved. These are deterministic opening-shot benchmarks, not human win rates or proof of enjoyable difficulty. Human playtesting remains necessary for balance.

Earlier prototype headless Chromium 90-frame sample: median 16.7 ms, p95 17.6 ms; zero layout events during the sample; 8.4 ms total script time. This predates the strategic opponent and is not a current performance claim, hardware-wide guarantee or full paint trace. Real-device touch testing remains outstanding.

The Apple-design and animation-systems guidance informed immediate input, explicit pause controls, transform-only sprite movement and no decorative loops. Impeccable adaptation guidance informed upright controls and mobile viewport sizing. Simulation uses fixed 240 Hz substeps with clamped elapsed time and a speed-limited opponent. Art is not an exact extraction of the original concept; refer to the asset pack fidelity notes. The game uses simple circle collisions and rectangular walls; rounded-corner collision geometry and opponent difficulty tuning can follow user playtesting.
