## Scope and material limit

The approved stills contain no reusable 3D meshes. Procedurally modelled illustrative figures establish blocking for this playable prototype; their detail is not equivalent to the rendered concept characters. This was disclosed before implementation. The owner explicitly rejected the toy-like quality as final art. Characters, environments, materials and lighting require an authored mature asset pass after prototype review, followed by full production checks. This prototype must not be described as production-ready. Do not use a flat concept image as the moving 3D world.

Use one Three.js renderer loaded only here, native scrolling and dialogs, existing UI tokens and icons. Three.js and its typings are the only added packages (MIT; no paid service). No arbitrary control framework or physics engine. Native scrolling is not cancelled or wheel-hijacked; a short time-based response smooths scene progress. Native scroll and autoplay use the same per-passage weights. Phones stay subordinate. Later chapters have separate furnished places and people for product work, team collaboration, engineering, a talk and audience, mentoring, the KubeCon booth, Swiggy, Quivly and the public-tool/Altr/Tethr workspaces. Desks, notebooks, microphones, workshop objects and public artifacts support those activities. Website source inspectors include provenance and honest project stages.
